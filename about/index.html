About Honoring Our Heroes

It started at a kitchen table.

Our mission is to honor and provide emotional aid to veterans and their families. Veterans are defined as military personnel — active, retired, or honorably discharged.

Our Story · In Judy's Words

From 30 veterans to more than 600 guests.

In 2011, I was asked by the ER (Exalted Ruler) to be her Veterans Services Chairman. Given free rein and a $1,000 budget, I decided to have a steak dinner to honor our 30 veterans and their significant others. We had a State Representative, who was also an Air Force veteran, as our keynote speaker. It was a great success — the veterans came to me saying they felt special and appreciated. We continued that tradition for the next few years.

I was then asked to be the District Veterans Services Chairman. I hesitated, but relented when the new ER, Marty Carroll, asked me if I'd like to make a bigger event. I said I would, but I'd need more grant money. He said, "You plan it, and I'll find you the money." The idea was born over the kitchen table with my husband Ed. I wanted to thank and praise every veteran in our little town of 40,000.

We rented a tent, tables, and chairs, and had Mission BBQ cater the event. Local businesses pitched in with coffee, tea, and sodas. We printed free tickets and invited our beloved veterans, some from the Tampa Veterans Hospital, the Mayor, and the County Commissioner. They came with proclamations, support, and enthusiasm. The mayor turned to me and said, "This is a great day, and we must continue to honor our veterans every year." And so, it began.

We had around 160 that first year, and it has since grown to over 600. We've had dynamic speakers every year. In 2019, State Senator Danny Burgess told us we now have the largest indoor event for veterans in the State of Florida.

Without the generosity of the Elks Grant, local businesses, and individuals, this event would not be possible. And without our marvelous volunteers — including Boy Scout Troop 744, Elks Lodge members, Suncoast Credit Union, and many others — it would not be possible to make this happen.

A thank-you at the diner

A Yellow Ribbon · 2019

Tie a yellow ribbon 'round the old oak tree.

In 2019 we became aware that 22 veterans take their own lives every day. We made that year's theme the old anthem of homecoming — and high school honor students, Boy Scouts, and volunteers tied yellow ribbons around trees across town.

City Hall's phones nearly shut down from neighbors who thought the marked trees were going to be cut down. Once the real reason came out, all was forgiven — and the message was impossible to miss.

If you or a veteran you know is struggling, call the Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988, then press 1. Available 24/7.

It's all about the dash.

Two dates on every veteran's headstone, separated by one small mark. Inside that mark is the enlistment, the deployment, the homecoming, the long ordinary years that followed. Everything we do is for the dash.
